# 🎯 Strategic Campaigns

**Long-term organizing for structural change on your campus.**

---

## What Are Strategic Campaigns?

**Strategic campaigns** are sustained, multi-month efforts aimed at achieving concrete institutional change. Unlike one-off events or weekly actions, these campaigns:

- Have clear, specific goals
- Build power over time
- Require coalition building
- Create lasting change
- Take months or years

**Three main types:**
1. **Divestment Campaigns** - Get universities to divest from occupation
2. **Speaker Programs** - Bring Palestinian voices and experts to campus
3. **Media Engagement** - Challenge bias, shift narratives

---

## 🏦 Divestment Campaigns

**Goal:** Get your university to divest from companies profiting from Israeli occupation and apartheid.

**Timeline:** 1-3 years typically  
**Difficulty:** High (but achievable!)  
**Impact:** Major institutional change

### Why Divestment?

**Economic pressure:**
- BDS (Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions) is Palestinian-led strategy
- Modeled after anti-apartheid movement
- Economic pressure for policy change

**Educational value:**
- Exposes university complicity
- Builds awareness on campus
- Creates organizing infrastructure

**Moral clarity:**
- Universities shouldn't profit from occupation
- Aligns investments with stated values
- Student power in action

### Canadian Precedents

**Recent wins:**
- **Montreal Student Strike (2024)** - 60 associations, 46,000+ students struck for divestment
- **York University Faculty Association** - Passed divestment motion
- **University of Ottawa professors** - Divestment motion
- Multiple student unions passed resolutions

## Phase 1: Research (2-3 months)

### Step-by-Step Research Process

**1. Form Research Working Group**
- 3-5 dedicated people
- Assign research coordinator
- Set regular meetings
- Divide research tasks

**2. Request University Investment Information**

**Methods:**
- **Freedom of Information (FOI) requests** - Legal right to public documents
- **Public documents** - Annual reports, board minutes, endowment reports
- **University website** - Investment policy, board information
- **Direct requests** - Email finance office, president's office

**What to request:**
- Complete list of endowment holdings
- Investment managers used
- Investment policy documents
- Board of Governors financial reports
- Research partnerships and contracts

**FOI Tips:**
- Be specific but broad enough
- Request last 3-5 years
- Allow 30-60 days for response
- Appeal if denied
- Document everything

**3. Identify Problematic Investments**

**Target companies:**
- Weapons manufacturers (Lockheed Martin, Raytheon, Elbit Systems)
- Tech companies enabling surveillance (Palantir, Microsoft Azure for military)
- Banks financing settlements (TD, RBC if directly involved)
- Companies operating in settlements
- Construction companies building on occupied land

**Use these databases:**
- **[Who Profits](https://www.whoprofits.org/)** - Comprehensive database
- **[Investigate by AFSC](https://investigate.afsc.org/)** - Investment research tool
- **[BDS Movement targets](https://bdsmovement.net/)**
- **Canadian BDS Coalition resources**

**4. Document Everything**

**Create spreadsheet with:**
- Company name
- Amount invested (if available)
- What company does
- Why it's problematic (with sources)
- International law violations
- Comparable divestment precedents
- Alternative investments

**Example row:**
| Company | Investment Amount | Activity | Violations | Precedent |
|---------|------------------|----------|------------|-----------|
| Elbit Systems | $X million | Weapons manufacturer, surveillance tech for Israeli military | Geneva Conventions Art. 33 (collective punishment) | UK unis divested |

**5. Research Precedents**

**Document:**
- Other Canadian universities that divested
- Successful campaign strategies used
- How administration responded
- Media coverage
- Timeline from launch to win
- Obstacles encountered and overcome

**Case studies:**
- McGill divestment campaign
- Concordia history
- US university wins
- UK university divestment movement

## Phase 2: Coalition Building (Ongoing)

### Internal Organizing

**Build Core Team (10-15 committed members)**

**Structure:**
- Campaign coordinator
- Research working group
- Communications team
- Outreach team
- Action/events team

**Regular activities:**
- Weekly meetings
- Clear roles (see [Role Templates](/campus-organizing/roles))
- Shared governance
- Decision-making process
- Communication channels (Signal group)

### Faculty and Staff

**Strategy:**
1. **Identify sympathetic faculty**
   - Ask around, look for Palestine scholars
   - Check who signed past statements
   - Look at research interests
   - Consider department culture

2. **Meet individually first**
   - Build genuine relationships
   - Share your research
   - Ask for advice
   - Don't demand immediate public support

3. **Request faculty statement**
   - Draft statement supporting divestment
   - Grounded in academic freedom and ethics
   - Collect signatures
   - Powerful when released publicly

4. **Connect with faculty unions**
   - Present at union meeting
   - Request endorsement
   - Coordinate on joint actions
   - Leverage union power

5. **Engage supportive staff**
   - Librarians, counselors, staff unions
   - Broader coalition

### Student Government

**Approach:**
1. **Present to student council**
   - Request time on agenda
   - Professional presentation
   - Share research
   - Answer questions

2. **Seek endorsement resolution**
   - Draft clear resolution
   - Find sponsors on council
   - Lobby councillors individually
   - Pack meeting with supporters
   - Be prepared for opposition

3. **Request funding**
   - Campaign operating costs
   - Event budgets
   - Use legitimacy for access

4. **Coordinate on actions**
   - Joint events
   - Mutual support
   - Amplification

### Other Student Groups

**Key partners:**
- Climate justice groups (fossil fuel divestment allies)
- Indigenous solidarity (shared analysis of settler colonialism)
- Anti-racism organizations
- Religious/cultural groups
- Labor solidarity groups
- Graduate student associations

**Coalition structure:**
- Regular coalition meetings
- Shared statement of solidarity
- Co-sponsored events
- Mutual aid and support
- Respect autonomy

### Community Organizations

**Off-campus partners:**
- Local Palestine solidarity groups
- Faith communities (churches, mosques, synagogues with progressive members)
- Labor unions
- Alumni networks
- Community groups

**Benefits:**
- Broader pressure on administration
- Resources and expertise
- Media connections
- Long-term sustainability

## Phase 3: Public Launch (1-2 months)

### Petition

**Elements of strong petition:**
- **Clear demands** - Specific companies, timeline
- **Research-backed rationale** - Why these companies
- **Moral and legal framework** - International law, university values
- **Feasible alternatives** - Socially responsible investments exist
- **Coalition sign-ons** - Organizations endorsing

**Goal:** 1000+ signatures for credibility
- Diverse signatory base
- Students, faculty, staff, alumni, community

**Platforms:**
- Change.org for reach
- Paper petitions for visibility
- Both for maximum signatures

### Public Statement

**Co-signed by multiple organizations:**
- Student groups
- Faculty
- Community partners

**Content:**
- Clear demands with timeline
- Research summary
- Moral imperative
- Call to action

**Release:**
- Media advisory sent to campus/local press
- Posted on all platforms
- Delivered to administration
- Public reading at event

### Educational Campaign

**Weekly teach-ins:**
- What is divestment?
- Why these companies?
- How divestment works
- Student power in history
- Q&A sessions

**Social media content series:**
- Company profiles ("Did you know your tuition invests in...")
- Student testimonials
- Faculty support
- Counter opposition talking points
- Precedents and wins

**Op-eds in campus paper:**
- Multiple authors
- Different angles (moral, legal, economic, student power)
- Regular presence
- Respond to opposition

**Tabling with information:**
- Regular presence on campus
- Handouts with research
- Sign petition
- Recruit to campaign
- [Tabling guide](/campus-organizing/weekly-actions#tabling)

**Speaker series:**
- Palestinian voices
- Divestment organizers from other campuses
- International law experts
- Economists on ethical investment
- Former Israeli soldiers

### Meeting with Administration

**Request formal meeting:**
- Email president, board chair, finance VP
- Multiple signatories
- Professional tone
- Specific date request

**At the meeting:**
- Bring 5-10 representatives (diverse)
- Present research professionally
- Deliver petition physically
- State clear demands
- Propose timeline for response (30-60 days)
- Request follow-up meeting
- **Document everything** - notes, recording if allowed

**Follow-up:**
- Send thank you email
- Reiterate demands in writing
- Set deadline for response
- Make public if ignored

## Phase 4: Escalation (As Needed)

### Graduated Tactics

**Start with education, escalate as needed:**

**Level 1: Education and Pressure**
- Continued teach-ins
- Media coverage
- Petition growth
- Coalition building

**Level 2: Visibility Actions**
- Public demonstrations
- Attend board meetings (public comment)
- Banner drops
- Flash mobs
- Die-ins

**Level 3: Disruption (Non-violent)**
- Occupy administrative offices (during business hours, within rules)
- Coordinated walkouts
- Class cancellations
- Grade strikes (students refuse to submit grades)

**Level 4: Sustained Occupation**
- Encampments (tent cities on campus)
- Building occupations
- **HIGH RISK** - only if other tactics exhausted
- **Legal observer required**
- **Know your rights** - [Legal guide](/support/legal)

### Important Safety Considerations

**Before any action:**
☐ Legal observer present  
☐ Know Your Rights training for participants  
☐ Risk assessment completed  
☐ Contact Palestine Legal: [palestinelegal.org](https://palestinelegal.org/)  
☐ Ensure informed consent (participants know risks)  
☐ Safety plan (medical, de-escalation, exit strategy)  
☐ Communication plan  
☐ Media strategy  
☐ Legal support on call  

**Palestine Legal can provide:**
- Know Your Rights workshops
- Legal support if students face discipline
- Strategic advice
- Training on law enforcement interactions

## 🎤 Bringing Speakers to Campus

**Goal:** Educational events featuring Palestinian voices, scholars, activists, and experts.

**Timeline:** 3-6 months planning  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Impact:** Education, legitimacy, media attention

### Speaker Identification

**Categories:**

**Palestinian voices (prioritize):**
- Palestinian academics
- Palestinian activists and organizers
- Palestinian journalists
- Palestinian artists and poets
- Palestinian students from other campuses

**Scholars:**
- Rashid Khalidi, Noura Erakat, Ilan Pappé, Sara Roy
- International law experts
- Middle East historians

**Activists:**
- BDS movement organizers
- Human rights advocates
- Former Israeli soldiers speaking out (Breaking the Silence)

**Canadian experts:**
- Canadian MPs
- Canadian policy analysts
- Canadian Palestinian community leaders
- Canadian journalists who've reported from Palestine

**Jewish allies:**
- Jewish Voice for Peace speakers
- Independent Jewish Voices Canada
- Rabbis for Human Rights

### 3-Month Planning Process

**Month 1: Outreach**

**Week 1-2:**
- Identify speaker and confirm availability
- Check if honorarium required
- Discuss topic and format
- Confirm travel needs

**Week 3-4:**
- Send formal invitation
- Negotiate honorarium/travel budget
- Sign contract or agreement if needed
- Confirm date (give 2-3 options)
- Establish point of contact

**Month 2: Planning**

**Week 5-6:**
- Book appropriate venue (size based on expected attendance)
- Submit event through official university channels
- Arrange travel and accommodation
- Plan event format (lecture + Q&A typical)
- Recruit moderator (faculty member ideal)
- Find co-sponsors

**Week 7-8:**
- Security assessment (anticipate opposition?)
- Legal observer if needed
- Book equipment (projector, microphone, recording)
- Plan reception if budget allows
- Prepare briefing document for speaker

**Month 3: Promotion**

**Week 9-10:**
- Create event poster/graphics
- Social media campaign begins
- Print posters, distribute around campus
- Email to student groups, departments
- Pitch to campus media
- Create Facebook event

**Week 11-12:**
- Intensify promotion
- Class announcements (ask sympathetic profs)
- Chalking (if allowed)
- Co-sponsor organizations promote
- Media interviews with organizers
- Final logistics confirmed

**Week of Event:**
- Daily social media posts
- Email reminders
- Confirm all details with speaker
- Brief moderator
- Volunteer roles assigned
- Test all equipment

### Day-of Execution

**2 Hours Before:**
- Set up room (seating, stage, signs)
- Test AV thoroughly (laptop, projector, microphone)
- Set up book/literature table
- Set up sign-in sheet
- Prepare green room for speaker (water, snacks, wifi, bathroom access)

**30 Minutes Before:**
- Welcome speaker, show them space
- Brief moderator on timing, Q&A process
- Brief volunteers (greeters, AV, Q&A monitors)
- Legal observer briefed and positioned
- Start recording (if permitted)

**During Event:**
- Welcome and land acknowledgment
- Introduce speaker (brief bio)
- Speaker presentation
- Moderated Q&A (collect written questions or microphone runners)
- Closing remarks (call to action, upcoming events)
- Thank yous

**After Event:**
- Thank speaker personally
- Collect feedback forms
- Clean up space
- Post-event social (if planned)

### Follow-Up

**Within 24 hours:**
- Email thank you to speaker
- Thank volunteers and partners
- Post photos/highlights on social media (with permission)

**Within 1 week:**
- Share recording if permitted
- Media coverage compilation
- Thank you posts for co-sponsors
- Internal debrief (what worked, what to improve)

**Within 1 month:**
- Add speaker to database for future
- Update speaker toolkit
- Assess impact (how many attended, feedback, media reach)
- Follow up on action items from event

### Handling Opposition

**Anticipate:**
- Administration may scrutinize event
- Opposition groups may protest or try to shut down
- Media may frame negatively

**Prepare:**
- Know university free speech policies
- Have Palestine Legal contact ready
- Brief speaker on potential opposition
- Legal observer present
- De-escalation plan
- Record everything

**Respond:**
- Don't engage with hecklers during event
- Have moderator authority to maintain order
- If significant disruption, pause and ask for respect
- Document opposition (photos, video)
- Public statement affirming free speech

### Budget Considerations

**Typical costs:**
- Speaker honorarium: $500-$3000
- Travel: $200-$1500
- Accommodation: $100-$300
- Venue: Often free on campus
- Equipment: Often free on campus
- Promotion: $50-$200
- Reception: $100-$500

**Total: $1000-$5000** depending on speaker

**Funding sources:**
- Student union event grants
- Co-sponsor contributions
- Department funding (if academic speaker)
- Ticket sales (controversial, may limit access)

## 📰 Media Engagement Campaign

**Goal:** Challenge biased media coverage, promote accurate narratives, build relationships with journalists.

**Timeline:** Ongoing  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Impact:** Narrative shift, public education

### Why Media Matters

- **Shapes public opinion** - How Palestine is covered affects support
- **Legitimizes movements** - Good coverage helps organizing
- **Holds institutions accountable** - Media pressure works
- **Educates broader public** - Beyond campus reach

### Strategy: Three Levels

**1. Reactive** - Respond to biased coverage  
**2. Proactive** - Generate positive coverage  
**3. Sustained** - Build media relationships  

---

### Reactive: Challenging Biased Coverage

**When you see biased coverage:**

**Step 1: Document**
- Screenshot or save article
- Note specific problems (passive voice, lack of context, etc.)
- [Media bias guide](/education/media-literacy)

**Step 2: Respond**

**Letters to Editor:**
- Submit within 24-48 hours of article
- 150-250 words typically
- One clear point
- Local/personal angle
- Follow paper's submission guidelines

**Template:**
```
Dear Editor,

Regarding "[article title]" (Date), I was concerned by [specific issue].

[Explain problem with example from article]

[Provide accurate framing or missing context]

As a [student/reader/community member], I expect [newspaper] to provide balanced coverage that includes Palestinian voices and acknowledges international law.

Sincerely,
[Name], [City]
```

**Complaints:**
- [CJPME Media Accountability Project](https://www.cjpmemap.ca/) - Report biased coverage
- Campus paper ombudsman
- Broadcast standards council

**Social Media:**
- Quote tweet or post with correction
- Tag the outlet and journalist
- Provide sources
- Don't be abusive

**Step 3: Coordinate**

- Don't all send identical letters
- Coordinate on shared talking points
- Multiple letters increase chance of publication
- Different angles from different people

---

### Proactive: Generating Coverage

**Press Releases:**

**When to send:**
- Major events (speakers, protests, actions)
- New campaign launches
- Responses to university decisions
- Statements on current events

**How to write:**

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
[Date]

[HEADLINE - Clear and newsworthy]

[City, Province] – [Lead paragraph: Who, what, when, where, why]

[Quote from student organizer - personal, compelling]

[Additional context and details]

[Quote from another source if available]

[Background on organization]

[Contact information]

###
```

**Distribution:**
- Campus paper
- Local papers
- Student radio
- Community media
- Send 24-48 hours before event or morning of for statements
- Follow up with phone call

**Op-Eds:**

**How to pitch:**
- **Email opinion editor** with:
  - Brief description of piece (1-2 sentences)
  - Why it's timely
  - Your credentials/why you should write it
  - Word count (usually 600-800)

**Structure:**
- Strong opening hook
- Clear argument
- Evidence and examples
- Personal perspective
- Call to action or solution
- Compelling conclusion

**Increase chances:**
- Timely (tied to current events)
- Local angle
- Strong credentials
- Clear writing
- Unique perspective

**Media Advisories:**

**For events with visual elements:**
- Protests, rallies, vigils
- Send to photo editors and assignment desks
- Include: What, when, where, photo opportunities, spokesperson available

---

### Sustained: Building Media Relationships

**Identify Journalists:**
- Who covers campus?
- Who covers Middle East?
- Who's written fairly before?
- Who's written badly? (still engage, educate)

**Build Relationships:**
- Follow them on Twitter
- Send them stories (not just your own)
- Offer to be a source
- Meet for coffee (no ask, just relationship)
- Respond quickly when they reach out
- Be reliable and truthful

**Be a Good Source:**
- Return calls/emails promptly
- Provide accurate information
- Have sources ready
- Admit when you don't know
- Suggest other sources
- Don't expect control over story

**Media Training:**
- Practice interviews
- Develop key talking points
- Learn to bridge to your message
- Stay on message under pressure
- Record practice interviews

**Spokesperson Development:**
- Identify 2-3 strong communicators
- Provide media training
- Practice Q&A
- Prep for hostile questions
- Support after tough interviews

## 📊 Measuring Campaign Success

### Divestment Campaign Metrics

**Short-term:**
- Petition signatures (goal: 1000+)
- Coalition partners (goal: 10+ organizations)
- Event attendance
- Media coverage
- Social media reach
- Student government votes

**Medium-term:**
- Administration meetings secured
- Board of Governors attention
- Faculty statement signatures
- Community support
- Sustained mobilization

**Long-term:**
- Policy changes
- Investment reviews
- Partial divestment
- Full divestment
- Process changes (investment committee student seats, etc.)

### Speaker Event Metrics

- Attendance numbers
- Diversity of attendees
- Quality of Q&A
- Media coverage
- Social media reach
- Post-event survey feedback
- Follow-up engagement (new members, donations, etc.)
- Long-term: Speaker series established, regular programming

### Media Campaign Metrics

- Letters to editor published
- Op-eds published
- Press release pickup
- Media tone shift over time
- Journalist relationships built
- Campus paper editorial board meetings
- Balanced coverage increase
