# Watermelon Club Writing Style Guide

## Core Principles

### 1. **Call It What It Is**
This is a genocide. The UN has recognized it. Don't hedge, don't soften. The violence of the oppressed is never the same as the violence of the oppressor. We cannot destroy the master's house with the master's tools.

### 2. **KISS (Keep It Simple, Stupid)**
- Write for clarity, not sophistication
- Short sentences beat long ones
- If you can say it in 5 words instead of 15, do it
- Don't use corporate jargon or buzzwords

### 3. **DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself)**
- Make your point once, make it well
- Consolidate repetitive bullet points into clear prose
- Trust the reader to understand without over-explanation

### 4. **Don't Explain the Obvious**
- Readers are intelligent
- Don't patronize with excessive context
- Get to the point

### 5. **Center the "Why"**
- Start with purpose, not process
- Make the stakes clear
- Connect actions to liberation

## Writing Guidelines

### Tone
- **Frank & blunt**, not corporate
- **Revolutionary**, not reformist
- **Pragmatic**, not abstract
- **Direct**, not diplomatic

### Voice
- Active voice > passive voice
- First/second person when appropriate ("we," "you")
- Imperative mood for actions ("Do this" not "You should consider doing this")

### Structure
- **Explanatory sentences** > excessive bullet points
- Bullets work for lists of concrete items, links, or quick references
- For concepts, context, or instructions: write in sentences
- 2-3 explanatory sentences beats 10 one-line bullets

### Language Choices

**YES:**
- Genocide (it is)
- Occupation (it is)
- Apartheid (multiple human rights orgs confirm)
- Resistance (it's legitimate)
- Liberation (the goal)
- Complicit (when institutions are)

**NO:**
- "Conflict" (implies equal sides)
- "Complicated" (it's not)
- "Both sides" (false equivalence)
- "Concerns about" (weak language)
- "Stakeholders" (corporate speak)
- "Leverage" (unless literal)

### Example Transformations

❌ **Before (Corporate):**
```
In order to maximize impact and create sustainable change, stakeholders should consider leveraging strategic partnerships to facilitate meaningful dialogue around divestment opportunities.
```

✅ **After (Direct/Clear):**
```
Build coalitions with faculty unions and community groups. Together, pressure your university to divest.
```

❌ **Before (Over-bulleted):**
```
**Planning your event:**
- Choose a date
- Book a room
- Create event poster
- Share on social media
- Send email invites
- Set up chairs
- Prepare materials
- Arrive early
```

✅ **After (Explanatory):**
```
**Planning your event:**
Book your room 2-3 weeks ahead. Create a poster and blast it everywhere—social media, email lists, print copies for campus boards. Arrive 30 minutes early to set up.
```

❌ **Before (Hedging):**
```
Some organizations have raised concerns about potential human rights violations that may be occurring.
```

✅ **After (Frank):**
```
Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, and B'Tselem have documented Israeli apartheid and war crimes.
```

## Content Patterns

### Action-Oriented Sections
Start with **what** and **why**, then **how**:

```
## Tabling

Tabling puts you directly in front of students who don't know what's happening in Palestine. You'll have 30-second conversations that can change minds.

Set up a table in a high-traffic area. Bring:
- Flyers with QR codes to resources
- A petition (physical and digital)
- Laptop showing documentary clips
- Sign-up sheet for your group

Talk to people. Don't lecture. Ask questions: "Do you know where your tuition is invested?"
```

### Resource Lists
Keep bullets for actual lists. Add brief context only when necessary:

```
## Documentaries

- **Five Broken Cameras** (2011) - Oscar-nominated, follows Palestinian farmer's nonviolent resistance
- **Al-Nakba** (2008) - 4-part series on the 1948 expulsion
- **1948: Creation & Catastrophe** (2017) - Parallel Israeli and Palestinian narratives
```

### Guides & How-Tos
Mix sentences and bullets based on complexity:

```
## Starting a Divestment Campaign

Research takes 2-3 months. File Freedom of Information requests to see where your university invests. Look for defense contractors, companies operating in illegal settlements, or those on BDS lists.

Once you have the data:
1. Build a coalition (10-15 committed people minimum)
2. Launch a petition (aim for 1000+ signatures)
3. Request a meeting with administration
4. Go public if they refuse

If they stonewall you, escalate. Hold rallies, occupy spaces, coordinate walkouts. Make it impossible to ignore.
```

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Corporate Speak
❌ "Facilitate meaningful engagement"
✅ "Talk to people"

❌ "Leverage strategic partnerships"
✅ "Work with allies"

❌ "Stakeholder buy-in"
✅ "Get people on board"

### Over-Explanation
❌ "It's important to remember that organizing takes time and patience, and you shouldn't feel discouraged if things don't happen immediately."
✅ "Organizing is slow. Stay persistent."

### False Balance
❌ "While there are different perspectives on the situation..."
✅ "This is occupation. It's illegal under international law."

### Passive Construction
❌ "Actions should be taken by students to hold universities accountable."
✅ "Students: hold your university accountable."

## Final Check

Before publishing, ask:
1. Did I call the genocide a genocide?
2. Would a first-year student understand this?
3. Did I cut all corporate jargon?
4. Are my bullet points actual list items, or should they be sentences?
5. Is this as short as it can be while staying clear?
6. Did I center liberation, not just reform?

---

**Remember:** We're not writing corporate DEI statements. We're organizing for Palestinian liberation. Write accordingly.
